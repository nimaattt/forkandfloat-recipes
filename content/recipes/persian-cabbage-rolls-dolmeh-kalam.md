dietaryTags: ""
ingredients:
  - name: "Green cabbage"
    amount: "1 large"
  - name: "Ground beef or soy chunks"
    amount: "1 cup"
  - name: "Onion or shallot"
    amount: "1 small, finely chopped"
  - name: "Garlic"
    amount: "3 cloves, minced"
  - name: "Split peas"
    amount: "1/2 cup"
  - name: "Persian rice or jasmine rice"
    amount: "1 cup"
  - name: "Bell pepper"
    amount: "1 small or 1/2 large, finely diced"
  - name: "Fresh parsley"
    amount: "1 cup, finely chopped"
  - name: "Olive oil"
    amount: "1/2 cup"
  - name: "Turmeric powder"
    amount: "1 tsp"
  - name: "Smoked paprika"
    amount: "1 tsp"
  - name: "Cumin powder"
    amount: "1 tsp"
  - name: "Salt"
    amount: "to taste"
  - name: "Freshly ground black pepper"
    amount: "to taste"
  - name: "Tomato paste"
    amount: "1 cup"
  - name: "Simple tomato sauce"
    amount: "2 cups"
steps:
  - title: "Soak the ingredients"
    body: "Place the split peas, rice, and soy chunks, if using, in three separate bowls. Cover each with water and let them soak while you prepare the cabbage and remaining ingredients."
  - title: "Prepare the cabbage"
    body: "Using a sharp knife, carefully remove the hard core from the cabbage. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil, the pot should be large enough to fit the whole cabbage. Add the cabbage and simmer for about 10 minutes, or until the leaves have softened and are flexible enough to roll."
  - title: "Start the filling"
    body: "Heat a drizzle of olive oil in a large pan over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and cook until translucent. Add the minced garlic and cook briefly until fragrant."
  - title: "Add the split peas and tomato paste"
    body: "Drain the soaked split peas and add them to the pan. Cook for a couple of minutes. Create an empty space in the center of the pan and add the tomato paste directly to the hot surface. Fry the tomato paste for a couple of minutes to deepen its flavor, then mix it into the onions and split peas."
  - title: "Add the rice"
    body: "Drain the soaked rice and add it to the pan. Pour in 2 cups of water, cover, and simmer for about 15 minutes, stirring occasionally."
  - title: "Cook the beef or soy"
    body: "In a separate pan, heat a little olive oil. Drain the soy chunks, if using, or add the ground beef directly to the hot pan. Season with turmeric, smoked paprika, cumin, salt, and freshly ground black pepper. Cook for a few minutes until browned and cooked through, then add it to the rice mixture."
  - title: "Finish the filling"
    body: "Add the finely diced bell pepper and chopped parsley. Mix everything together well, then remove from the heat."
  - title: "Separate the cabbage leaves"
    body: "Remove the cabbage from the water and allow it to cool enough to handle. Carefully separate the softened leaves without tearing them. Trim away the thickest part of the center stem from each leaf to make rolling easier."
  - title: "Fill and roll"
    body: "Lay a cabbage leaf flat and spoon some of the filling into the center. Fold the bottom of the leaf over the filling, tuck in both sides, then continue rolling tightly to enclose the filling. Repeat with the remaining cabbage leaves and filling."
  - title: "Layer the cabbage rolls"
    body: "Add a thin layer of tomato sauce to the bottom of the same large pot used for the cabbage. Arrange a layer of cabbage rolls over the sauce, add another layer of tomato sauce, and continue layering until all the rolls are in the pot."
  - title: "Simmer"
    body: "Cover and cook over medium-low heat for about 15 to 20 minutes, allowing the cabbage rolls to absorb the tomato sauce and all the flavors to come together."
howToServe:
  - "Serve hot with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil."
  - "Finish with fresh parsley and freshly ground black pepper."
  - "Enjoy cold or at room temperature as well."
  - "Serve on its own or alongside yogurt and fresh herbs."
chefsNote: "For a completely vegan version, use soy chunks instead of ground beef. 
These cabbage rolls are especially good for meal prep because the flavors continue to develop as they sit. Store them in the refrigerator and reheat as needed, or freeze them in an airtight container for up to 2 months."
